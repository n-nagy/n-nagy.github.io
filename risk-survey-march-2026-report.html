01 — Human Risk Scoring Importance

Strong demand, but divided on why it matters

Avg 8.0/10 across 47 practitioners — with sharp contrasts between believers and skeptics.

68% gave it an 8 or higher. The believers frame it as a capability gap — they can't tell who the most vulnerable person in their org is without this data. Skeptics aren't rejecting the concept; they're skeptical of current accuracy and signal-to-noise at scale.

Rated 10/10 "All employees are targets. Not just executives. Threat intel specifically to the employees or org is always a priority." expree.org / eip.tech
Rated 6/10 "Any employee can be a risk so scanning all of them might not be helpful — or too much information that we don't care about." sendiks.com

02 — How Practitioners Define "High Risk"

What someone does matters more than who they are

Four consistent lenses emerge — behavioral signals dominate, but access level and role exposure are close behind.

Most Common

Security Awareness & Behavior

Frequently fails phishing tests, ignores security protocols, consistently late on or fails training. An indifference to cyber hygiene. "Unaware of risks, doesn't follow protocols, clicks on things they shouldn't."

PhishingTrainingHygiene
Most Common

Privilege & Access

Elevated access, high privileges, or keys to critical systems or sensitive data. C-suite and IT admins frequently named. Blast radius is the core concept — not just behavior, but the damage they could cause.

IdPAccess ControlC-Suite
Common

Role & Exposure

Roles that can compromise security or financial integrity, or individuals with a high public profile targeted for social engineering, deepfakes, or BEC. Exposure matters independent of behavior.

BECSocial Engineering
Common

Policy Non-Compliance

Not following procedures, signing up for unsanctioned SaaS, sharing data without authorization. "Carelessly or maliciously accessing sensitive data" was the framing from several respondents.

Shadow ITDLPCompliance
Privileged access lens (rated 9/10) "A high risk employee to us is someone that carelessly or maliciously accesses sensitive data. My biggest worries are those signing up for unsanctioned apps — shadow IT — where they get breached and cause us to get compromised." Survey respondent
Contextual lens (rated 7/10) "I'd like to see control over how risk is assigned based on role. A department head should not be penalized for needing admin rights — context should change how a signal gets weighted." sendiks.com

Security control recommendations (62%) edge out training (57%) as the #1 automation ask — notable because it requires IdP integration and is more technically complex. Training automation is the cleaner first implementation.
The 21% with no process

One in five practitioners identify high-risk employees and do nothing systematic about it — not because they don't care, but because they lack a defined workflow. For this segment, automation doesn't create efficiency, it creates a capability they don't have at all.

The 43% training manually

The most common structured response is manually assigning training. This is the highest-signal group for training automation — they're already doing the behavior, they just want it to happen automatically based on score triggers.

Wants automation (rated 9/10) "Full circle automation is key. Sometimes tools, as well meaning as they are, cause more work in the end." thedi.ca
Capacity-blocked (rated 8/10) "Never. We need to review risk scores more frequently. We just don't have the time to follow through on anything we find." thedi.ca
