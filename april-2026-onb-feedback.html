02 — The Kickoff Call

Customers love the call. The ones who love it most got things done during it.

The kickoff call has near-universal approval. But not all kickoffs are equal — the responses that describe the best experiences consistently share one thing: integrations and setup happened live, not as post-call homework. Customers who just got a walkthrough rated lower than customers who left the call with the product running.

Individual CSMs are named by name in a significant share of 10/10 responses — Robert Nelson, Zach, Jack LeeHoffman, Kate, Soyoung, Emily, Reegan, Vitor appear repeatedly. Customers credit the person as much as the process. That is both the strength and the fragility of the current model.

"Do it together" calls score better than walkthroughs
Summit Utilities (10/10): "walked us through integrations on the call — we were able to get employees synced and SSO set up." Copado (9/10): "able to get Okta SSO, SCIM provisioning, and API services set up in the call." MX (9/10): "during the call we were able to work on implementation, which sets us up to start working right away." The pattern is consistent: live setup during the call correlates with stronger scores and more confident post-call language.
The expectation gap: walkthroughs vs. workshops
Several customers expected to do integrations on the call and discovered they were just talking about them. Compass Church (9/10): "I was told to have someone available for the Microsoft 365 integration portions — we talked about it, but we never actually did any integrations. I thought we would have." Bentley University (8/10): "Setting up our SSO and SCIM ingestion during the call would have been more helpful." The call format isn't always set to the right expectation.
Self-serve capable customers still value the call
Several customers who set up the product themselves without needing guidance still gave 10/10 and credited the call. FirstMark Capital: "I ended up setting a lot of the configurations myself since it's so intuitive — but Samantha was great and showed me things I missed." Metropolitan Family Services: "The UI is so intuitive I haven't needed help — but having someone available mattered." The call is additive even when it isn't load-bearing.

03 — Integrations and SCIM

Integration failures are the most concrete blocker in the first week. They also suppress scores disproportionately.

SCIM issues and missing IdP support show up before customers have run a single campaign. When they hit a wall here, there's often no clear recovery path — they wait on support or do it manually. A small number of customers had the opposite experience: they got integrations done live on the kickoff call and gave those sessions the highest marks.

Okta SCIM failures are the most-named specific blocker
Multiple customers cite SCIM import failing with Okta. This surfaces during setup, not later — meaning it's one of the first experiences a new customer has after the kickoff call ends. Alloy described it plainly: "SCIM fails to import data from Okta." When this breaks, there's no documented self-recovery path visible to the customer.
EntraAD SCIM provisioning is a consistent gap
Multiple respondents across different companies named EntraAD SCIM provisioning as absent or unreliable. Gonzaga University named it as the primary thing Adaptive could do to help them succeed. For Microsoft-heavy environments this is a table-stakes requirement, and the absence creates an awkward first week.
Support response time amplifies the problem
FSC (8/10): "We had SCIM integration issues and had to wait a long time for the support team to respond. We expect at least a response within 1–2 hours." When an integration breaks early in setup and there's no fast path to resolution, the customer's first days with the product are blocked. This is a compounding problem: the integration issue itself is one thing, but the wait for support is what shapes the experience.
Getting integrations done on the call is a strong positive signal
Several 10/10 respondents specifically credit live integration setup on the kickoff call. Copado: "able to get our Okta integration, SCIM provisioning, and API services all set up during the call — our IT person said the documentation was really good and easy to follow." This is the counter-pattern to the SCIM failure experience, and it suggests the integration experience is fixable within the current call model.

04 — What Good Onboarding Looks Like

Fast, human, and technically competent. The best experiences leave customers ready to run.

10/10 responses describe the same pattern: a short call, a knowledgeable CSM who respected their time, clear documentation, and a platform that was intuitive enough to navigate independently after the call. When all of these are present, customers score perfectly and describe the experience in enthusiastic terms.

Fast time to value
"Setup was a cinch so far, unlike other 3rd party apps." Speed is cited as a genuine differentiator, especially vs. KnowBe4. Customers feel they can be operational quickly when things work.
Knowledgeable, present CSM
Robert Nelson, Zach, Jack, Kate, Soyoung, Emily, Reegan each named multiple times in 10/10 responses. The human layer drives scores as much as the product itself.
Clear, upfront documentation
"I like the links to setting up SAML, SCIM, DMI being provided upfront." Customers who found docs comprehensive could self-serve after the call and felt supported without needing to ask.
Platform intuition after kickoff
"Very intuitive — the best products are those where training to use them is minimal." Customers in this camp needed the call to orient, not to learn. The platform does the rest.

05 — Where Onboarding Falls Short

The post-kickoff cliff. Customers leave the call and hit a wall.

Lower scores cluster around a consistent experience: the kickoff call was described positively, but customers felt unsupported or underprepared once it ended. Integration blockers, uncovered use cases, and AI features that required the Adaptive team to unlock all surface in the first days after setup.

No structured path after the call ends
The most common pattern in 7–8/10 responses: the kickoff was positive, but implementation felt self-directed with no clear second step. "Follow this guide, good luck." Customers who hit a problem — a broken SCIM sync, a use case not covered on the call, a missing integration — had no obvious escalation path. The call ends and the experience fragments.
AI features feel gated behind the team
Customers who were sold on deepfakes and AI capabilities discovered during onboarding that using them requires submitting a request to the Adaptive team. This mismatch between the demo and day-one access is a real frustration for customers who chose Adaptive specifically for those features. Several noted the gap directly, and it shapes how they rate the onboarding even when the rest of the call went well.
Use cases beyond the basics weren't covered
Several customers noted the kickoff covered phishing and training well but didn't address their specific setup: shared mailboxes, unique IdP configurations, compliance requirements, data access questions. They discovered gaps on their own and had to find their way to support. One customer recommended starting the call by discussing roles and responsibilities — who owns what after the call ends.

06 — What Success Looks Like to Customers

Customers define success as behavioral change, not platform activation.

When asked what they need to see to consider Adaptive a success, customers point to outcomes downstream of the platform: reduced phishing click rates, employees who recognize and report attacks, and a program that runs without constant admin effort. The onboarding experience needs to build a clear bridge from setup to those outcomes.

01
Measurable reduction in employee risk
Reduced phishing click rates, improved simulation pass rates, declining risky-user counts. Customers want a trend line they can show leadership. The most sophisticated respondents want this to tie to real-world reporting, not just sim performance.

07 — What Customers Need From Adaptive to Succeed

Support, check-ins, and content freshness. Not more features — more partnership.

When asked what Adaptive can do to help them succeed, customers did not primarily ask for new capabilities. They asked for ongoing responsiveness, proactive check-ins, best practice guidance, and evidence that the content will keep pace with the threat landscape.

Regular check-ins and a proactive cadence
Quarterly reviews, bi-monthly calls, and structured post-launch check-ins appeared in dozens of responses. Customers are not asking for hand-holding — they want a cadence that surfaces problems before they fester and keeps them current on capabilities they may not know exist. Several specifically asked for this to be scheduled, not reactive.
Best practices, not just product support
Customers want to know what a well-run program looks like. "Provide ongoing benchmarks to what other organizations our size are doing." Several asked for curated recommendations: which templates to start with, how to structure a first campaign, how to handle repeat clickers. This is as much a CSM problem as a product problem.
Content that evolves with the threat landscape
Content freshness came up in both concerns and in this section. Customers need to tell employees "this is what attackers are doing right now." Content that feels dated undermines the program internally. Several asked for an explicit new-content cadence or a newsletter-style feed of what's been added.

08 — Product Gaps That Surface During Onboarding

Self-serve deepfakes, SCIM gaps, and reporting all show up before customers have run a single campaign.

These are not distant product requests — they are things customers discover within the first days of setup. The top ask is the ability to create deepfake content without involving the Adaptive team. The second is SCIM reliability. Both directly suppress onboarding scores when absent.

01
Self-Service Deepfake Creation
The most frequently requested capability, and the clearest onboarding gap. Customers are sold on deepfakes, then discover they need to submit a request to Adaptive to actually create one. Several felt dependent on the team for the flagship feature. The ask: upload materials, generate a video, launch the sim — all without leaving the platform.
DeepfakesSelf-serveDay-1 gap
02
SCIM and Integration Reliability
Okta SCIM failures and missing EntraAD support are the most-named technical blockers. These appear during setup, not later. JumpCloud, Rippling, and Dayforce also surface as missing. Customers who hit these walls either wait on support or do manual workarounds — both erode early trust.
SCIMEntraADSetup blocker
03
Exec-Ready Reporting Out of the Box
Customers who onboard and then try to show results to leadership hit the reporting gap almost immediately. CSV exports require manual work before they're presentable. The ask is specific: PDF summaries, automated email reports, or an API to pull data into existing workflows. Also a retention risk — customers who can't show ROI are harder to renew.

The immediate effect: they can't launch a campaign that feels meaningfully different from their last vendor. The ask is breadth and freshness: seasonal, industry-specific, emerging threat-based templates.
